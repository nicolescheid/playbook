# Teaching and Training Principles

Nic's approach to designing anything meant to teach people: courses, workshops, talks, and AI enablement and capability work. It is her personal approach, not tied to an employer or a single project, and it applies wherever she teaches or presents.

Last reviewed: 2026-10-21. Status: provisional. The human-prompt formats below are still being developed.

---

## The aim

Get people thinking for themselves first, and thinking with AI second. The goal is to reactivate people's own thinking and build the skills they need, so that AI extends their thinking instead of replacing it. If a session introduces AI before people have thought for themselves, they can fall into letting AI do the thinking.

## Principles

1. **Human first, AI second.** Sequence every session so participants think before AI is introduced. Use human prompts (see below) for that step, not chat prompts.
2. **Earn attention on purpose.** In an attention economy, capturing attention is the first step of teaching. Use techniques that work with how people attend and learn: a curiosity gap, a surprise, a prediction before a reveal, a chance to generate an answer first. Different people respond to different techniques (some to the surprise, some to the unanswered question), so design for a spread of responses rather than assuming one.
3. **Every hook must be load-bearing.** Test: if the technique is removed, does the content get weaker? The payoff should be the content itself, and it should arrive soon. If a technique holds attention without delivering anything, cut it.
4. **Vary the technique.** Do not use the same technique repeatedly, within a session or across sessions. No formulas or templates. Vary both content and technique with the audience, the context and the moment. Her reasoning: relying on one technique leaves other kinds of attention and thinking unused.
5. **Serve the learner, and design the whole.** Techniques are used responsibly, in service of the learner's understanding. Plan the session as one designed whole, not as a sequence of individually effective tricks.

## Framing

Her analogy: a good diet depends on variety. Ultra-processed food delivers stimulation without nourishment. Its training equivalent is a technique that captures attention without delivering substance, or one that is repeated until it stops working. A good menu takes thought: who is eating, what suits the moment, and how the meal works as a whole.

## Human prompts

A human prompt asks participants to think on their own, before any AI use and before seeing other people's answers. It is not a chat prompt (a message typed into a chat window or to an AI).

Proposed formats, under evaluation and not yet tested:

- **Silent commit.** Participants write an answer on paper before anything is shared or revealed. Rationale: writing before sharing avoids anchoring on other people's answers.
- **Predict, then reveal.** Show the topic only, allow a short silence for a prediction, then reveal.
- **Written commitment at the close.** One thing they will do, or will catch themselves doing.

## Review checklist

- Do participants think for themselves before AI appears?
- Is each hook load-bearing? What does removing it cost?
- Is any technique repeated from earlier in the session or from a recent session?
- Is the technique mix chosen for this audience and moment, or is it a template?
- Does the whole session hold together?

## Research to lean on

Referenced by title and year. Check the papers before quoting specific findings.

- Loewenstein, G. (1994). The psychology of curiosity: A review and reinterpretation. *Psychological Bulletin*. Curiosity as a felt gap between what a person knows and what they want to know.
- Richland, L. E., Kornell, N., & Kao, L. S. (2009). The pretesting effect: Do unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance learning? *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*. Attempting answers before instruction, even wrong ones, can improve later learning.
- Slamecka, N. J., & Graf, P. (1978). The generation effect: Delineation of a phenomenon. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Learning and Memory*. Information a person generates themselves is remembered better.

## Using this with an AI assistant

Reference this file rather than copying it. Where a prompt block is needed in a project, paste this one inline:

```
When designing or reviewing training, workshops, talks or AI enablement content, apply these principles. Sequence human thinking before AI use. Check that every hook is load-bearing (removing it weakens the content). Flag any technique repeated from earlier in the session or from recent sessions, and any step where AI is introduced before participants have thought for themselves. Vary technique with the audience and moment. Do not produce a formula or template.
```

# Voice spec template

A template for specifying one person's or one product's voice — as distinct from [ai-writing-style-guide.md](ai-writing-style-guide.md), which is general anti-AI-tell guidance. That guide is portable across any writer; this one is filled in once per person or product and kept alongside their other reference material.

Copy the structure below and replace every placeholder. Keep it short — a voice spec that's longer than the guide it extends has lost the plot.

---

## Register and spelling

One line: formality level, regional spelling, and person (first/third).

> Example: Conversational but precise. British spelling. First person singular — never "we" for one person's work.

## Punctuation

Any rule stricter than the general guide. The general guide says moderate em-dashes; a personal spec is free to ban a mark outright if it's become a tell in your own writing.

> Example: No em dashes at all, not even load-bearing ones — use a colon or a full stop instead. Oxford comma always. Contractions throughout, including in headings.

## Affirmative voice

State that the antithesis rule applies (see [ai-writing-style-guide.md § The antithesis rule is really a voice rule](ai-writing-style-guide.md#the-antithesis-rule-is-really-a-voice-rule)), and name anything specific to this voice — a topic this person tends to frame negatively out of habit, for instance.

> Example: Say what something is, not what it isn't. Watch for this especially when describing setbacks — the habit is to frame them as "not a failure, but," when the accurate version is just to say what they are.

## Carve-out list

Phrases exempt from the antithesis rule. A carve-out earns its place when the comparison is the actual content and dropping half of it loses information, when the phrase is an established title or tagline, or when you say it out loud in that shape and it sounds like you.

> Example: "Not a roadmap, a bet" — this is the actual pitch for how the team plans, not decoration. Keep it.

## Signature phrases

Genuinely this person's or product's own turns of phrase, to be used sparingly — a reminder they exist, not a quota to hit.

> Example: "the quiet middle" (for the unglamorous stretch of a project after the initial push and before results show). Used maybe once per piece, only when it's the exact right fit.

## What to avoid

Short. Anything from the general guide that applies with extra force here, plus anything specific to this voice.

> Example: No forced metaphors. No rhetorical questions. No adverbs standing in for description.

## Restatement block

A short paragraph to paste near the end of a long prompt, where recency helps it hold (see [ai-writing-style-guide.md § Making it stick](ai-writing-style-guide.md#making-it-stick)).

> Example:
> ```
> Reminder on voice: first person, British spelling, no em dashes. State things in
> the affirmative — say what something is, don't define it against what it isn't.
> Exception: "not a roadmap, a bet" stays as written. Use "the quiet middle" only
> if it's the exact right fit, not by default.
> ```
